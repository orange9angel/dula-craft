# Director checklist (score every full draft; note time stamps)

1. Opening: frame 0 presentable (thumbnail); the first 10 s give a reason to keep watching.
2. Monotony: no single set/framing dominates >40% of the tile; a new image at least every section.
3. Rhythm: motion energy follows the song (quiet/loud); cuts land on beats; one rhythmic surprise (freeze, drop, hold).
4. Motion quality: nothing glides, jitters or jumps unless it is a deliberate cut.
4a. Travel direction (every moving figure, every shot): the figure's drawn facing (side-on, three-quarter, back)
    matches the direction of the surface it moves on at that spot of the plate. A side-on drawing only moves along a
    stretch that crosses the frame; on a road that recedes or curves away it needs a back or three-quarter drawing, or
    it must not travel there. The path is a polyline drawn along the surface, never a straight line between two screen
    points. A reviewer's note about this is a defect to fix, not a limitation to accept.
5. Physics/scale: placement plausible; nearer = bigger; props rest on what holds them; nothing blocks a used path.
6. Hierarchy: the lead is findable but not pasted-on; solid vs ghost used on purpose; colour reserved for the story.
7. Story beat: the key event (cause then effect) reads on one viewing; the ending lands on the last lyric.
8. Close-ups: alive (hair, light, passing world), not still pictures; faces consistent across ages/shots.
9. Earlier rejections recorded in the film's notes are not violated again after local repairs.
10. One memorable image per section; cut a device that does not serve the song.
11. Ground and sources: every figure's feet stand in the plate's walkable band; every painted source of motion (steam, flame,
    water, leaves) actually moves.
12. Lyrics and titles: font matches the film's look (checked in the ffmpeg fontselect log), readable on the brightest shot.

## Story episodes (add to the above)

13. Standalone: a first-time viewer states the lead's want, stakes, deadline and the world rule from the cut alone.
14. Laughs: a laugh or new comic image every 10-15 s; the stranger's "bored" and "got ahead" times are fixed or cut.
15. No preaching: no gag or running motif built on a virtue; nothing is framed as good behaviour rewarded.
16. Dialogue: every line sounds like that character in that moment (no announcing, no explaining the joke); no dead
    hold over ~2 s on a visible reaction; a change of topic is motivated by a change of expression.
17. Match comparable lines within 1 LU at the same distance and vocal effort. Check each perspective change
    against the sound script. Phrases stay intact and listeners can identify the source of a repeat or echo.
18. Score phrases resolve before speech, including tails. Check any sustained pad under speech against its
    written cue and the clarity of the words. Preserve deliberate silent beats and prior genre decisions.
19. Background sound: felt, not noticed - footsteps varied and low, birds sparse and distant, no audible loop, one
    ambience level per location, no unmotivated level jump (measure loudness per stem across the whole film), no pops.
    Attach the scene sound script, whole-scene listening notes and the comparison with the last accepted mix.
20. Continuity across parts: recurring locations, props and who-is-where match at every join.
