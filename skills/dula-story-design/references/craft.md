# Story craft for 2-5 minute films and music videos

## What has worked and what has not

- Worked: a clear comic engine with escalation and a reversal; one small human moment revealed slowly by the medium
  (a smile appearing through mist); exact callbacks that land on one viewing.
- Failed: stories with no highlight, plots that collapse after a good setup, action without people and setup/payoff,
  humour from ugliness or rustic clichés, formula premises, and any new piece that echoes an earlier one (a colour word
  in the title, the same rain and metro imagery and the same two-colour palette as the previous film, then "always just
  missing each other", called underpowered).

## Engines that fit a short

- **Rule + escalation + reversal**: one rule of the world, three escalating uses, the rule turned against itself.
- **Hidden whole**: the viewer sees fragments that only at the turn reveal they were one thing all along (the medium can
  carry this: shards, layers, sand, ink marks).
- **Wrong goal**: the hero pursues A; the film shows the cost; the hero picks B in the last beat.
- **Correspondence without meeting**: two characters communicate through a medium; the turn changes what the messages
  meant.
- **One object's journey**: an object passes through several lives; the last holder closes the loop with the first.
- **One person, two ages**: two figures the viewer takes for strangers turn out to be one person young and old.

Each needs: a hook inside 10 s, a turn the viewer cannot predict from the hook, and an ending image that pays off the
hook (same object, same place, changed meaning).

Play a callback once for a fresh viewer and ask which earlier words they recognized.
If they need a reminder, try keeping the phrase intact and changing who says it
or whom it describes. The new situation can supply the second meaning.

## Music video specifics

- One emotional arc, not a plot summary; 3-5 beats in total.
- The turn sits on a musical event (pre-chorus lift, drop, key change, bridge silence); the reveal on the biggest one.
- The medium must move with the music (for example pigment arriving on low-band energy).
- Avoid on-screen singing close-ups unless lip sync is solved.

## Clichés to refuse unless twisted

Always-just-missing each other; meet-cute then parting at a station; rain as the default sadness; it was all a dream;
the underdog wins the contest; the misunderstood monster; the letter never sent; amnesia as the reveal; a heart shape
as the climax image; a colour word as the title of a colour-led film.

## Titles

For a series, try the proposed name above several different episode summaries.
If it describes only the first incident, keep it for that episode. A recurring
place, pair of characters or lead with a distinctive epithet can carry the
series.

Good titles name a concrete thing from the story or a phrase a character could say (invented examples: "The
Umbrella Thief", "Seven Minutes Late", "A Song for the Lighthouse"). Rules:
1. A concrete image, object or line from this story, not the concept or the style ("color", "overlap", "fragments").
2. Creates a small question; does not explain the plot.
3. Short for a music video (1-4 words, or 2-6 characters in Chinese); sayable aloud; no rare characters.
4. No key word shared with an earlier title in the ledger; check synonyms too ("deviation" ~ "misalignment").
5. Draft eight or more, rank, and give the reason for the winner in one line.
