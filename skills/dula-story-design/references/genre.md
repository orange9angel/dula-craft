# Genre first: decide what kind of film this is

A film that has not chosen its genre plays as a list of incidents: things happen one after another, nobody knows
what the lead wants, and nothing is set up to pay off. Decide the genre before any premise, then build with that
genre's tools. Write the decision at the top of `story.md`.

## Step 0 - the genre card (fill all of it)

| Field | Answer in one line |
|---|---|
| Genre and form | comedy / drama / adventure / mystery / musical / MV; length; episode of a series or standalone |
| What the viewer should feel | laugh every 10-15 s / worry then relief / wonder / be moved at the end |
| Lead's want | a concrete goal the viewer can say in 5 words ("get the flower to her by the show") |
| Obstacle | what stands in the way, visible on screen |
| Stakes | what is lost if they fail, said or shown early |
| Clock | why now - a deadline the viewer can track (the show starts at 7, the bus leaves, the tide comes in) |
| Device | the world rule or running gag, shown AND said in character in the first ~40 s; every episode stands alone |

If any field is empty, the story will read as "flat, I don't know what they are doing". Fix the card first.

## Comedy toolkit (set up, turn, punch)

- **Every gag is a three-part unit**: setup (plant the expectation), escalation (repeat with a twist, usually three
  times), punch (the fourth beat breaks the pattern). Write gags as units, not as single funny images.
- **Comic persona**: the lead has one clear comic flaw or rule (too polite, too literal, always half a beat late).
  Every gag comes out of it. Side characters react in character (the straight man, the one who escalates).
- **Plant and pay off**: each act plants one object or line that returns later as a punch (callback). Map them:
  plant -> where it returns. A plant with no payoff is cut; a payoff with no plant is unearned.
- **Running gag** that changes meaning by the end (annoyance in act 1, rescue in the last act).
- **Reaction shots** carry half the laugh: cut to the witness after the punch, hold a beat.
- **Dialogue jokes land on the last word**; no explaining the joke afterwards; no one comments "that was funny".
- **Density**: a laugh or a new comic image every 10-15 s; one big set piece per act.
- Cold-read question for comedy: *where did you laugh, where did you get ahead of the joke, where were you bored?*

## Drama toolkit (time line, atmosphere, stakes)

- **Establish the normal first**: time of day, place, routine, who the lead is to the people around them; the
  viewer must know what "normal" is before it breaks.
- **Time line**: the viewer always knows when we are (morning of the show, an hour later, night) - clocks, light,
  shadows, crowds thinning. Each act moves the clock forward visibly.
- **Atmosphere**: light, weather, ambience and music set the mood before the event; the music has a theme that
  changes with the lead's state.
- **Stakes rise**: each act makes failure cost more; a midpoint turn changes the goal or the method; a low point
  ("all is lost") before the climax.
- **Emotional turn** is caused by the lead's own choice, on screen.
- Cold-read question for drama: *what did the lead want, when did you worry, what moved you, what did you not believe?*

## Long form (8-12 minutes): four acts

| Act | Share | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Set-up | ~25% | normal world, device shown and said, want + stakes + clock, inciting incident at the end |
| 2 Rising | ~25% | first plan; it works, then backfires; midpoint turn (new information or new method) |
| 3 Complication | ~25% | stakes highest, clock nearly out, low point caused by the lead's flaw |
| 4 Climax + button | ~25% | the flaw becomes the solution; payoffs of every plant; a short button gag or image |

Each act ends on a question the viewer wants answered. An act that only "continues the trip" is a defect.

## Music placement (story episodes are not musicals)

- Dialogue scenes run on continuous ambience and Foley, not on a score. A score under every line makes the film
  feel like a musical, and ducking it under speech makes it pump audibly.
- Place music only where it has a job: the opening, a transition or act end (a short sting), and one or two
  emotional beats with no dialogue on top. Each cue starts and ends on purpose.
- Mark the cue points in the beat sheet (music section column) before any music is generated.

## Dialogue

- Lines reveal character or advance the want; small talk and announcements ("today I will...") are cut.
- Exposition only through a character who has a reason to ask (a curious child, a newcomer).
- Read every line aloud in the character's voice: would a real person of that age say it, in that situation?
- Echoes, repeats and off-screen voices must be readable on one hearing (colour-coded subtitles, no brackets that
  look like thoughts).
